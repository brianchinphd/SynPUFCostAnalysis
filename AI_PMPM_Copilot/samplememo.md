1. Executive Summary
The Connecticut market showed a raw PMPM trend of -15.5% from 2008 to 2009. However, this decline masked a +5.4% increase in PMPM among members with stable 12-month coverage and a +7.1% risk-adjusted trend. Taken together, the results suggest underlying medical cost growth despite the apparent market-level decline.

2. QA / Interpretation Warning
The raw PMPM trend was likely distorted by a coverage-duration mix shift. Because of this denominator change, the market-level -15.5% trend should not be interpreted as a true improvement in underlying cost performance.

3. Key Trend Drivers
Within the stable cohort, the largest increases were observed in Carrier (+16.6%) and Outpatient (+14.0%) spend, while Inpatient spend declined (-5.5%). ER visits per member increased +21.1%, compared with only +5.9% growth in ambulance trips per member. This pattern is more consistent with increased walk-in ER utilization than with a broad rise in high-acuity emergency transport.

4. Recommended Follow-Up
Investigate potential walk-in ER leakage and redirect avoidable ED use where appropriate. Conduct a network coverage audit to assess whether access patterns or outpatient coverage dynamics contributed to the observed Carrier and Outpatient growth.
